# Bell Pepper Leaf Spot (Bacterial and Cercospora Spot) Management

Bell pepper leaf spot is commonly caused by the bacterium *Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria* (bacterial leaf spot) or the fungus *Cercospora capsici* (frogeye leaf spot). Both pathogens cause severe foliar necrosis, defoliation, and sunscald on developing capsicum fruit.

## Identification

Bacterial leaf spot begins as small, water-soaked, circular to irregular lesions on the lower leaf surface, turning dark brown with a paler, greasy or water-soaked border on the upper surface. In warm, wet conditions (24°C to 30°C with high relative humidity), the spots coalesce, causing leaves to turn chlorotic, roll, and drop prematurely. 
Cercospora leaf spot forms circular lesions with light grey or white centres and prominent dark brown halos, resembling a frogeye. Both diseases result in heavy defoliation, exposing peppers to direct sun and causing unmarketable sunscald.

## Distinguishing bacterial spot from fungal leaf spots

Bacterial spots appear water-soaked or translucent when held against backlight and lack the concentric circular rings typical of Alternaria or the distinct white papery centres of Cercospora. Bacterial exudate or streaming can be observed under low magnification when active lesions are excised into clean water droplets.

## Cultural control

Use certified disease-free pepper seeds and healthy seedlings. Treat seed with hot water (50°C for 25 minutes) or a 1% sodium hypochlorite soak to eliminate surface seed-borne bacteria. Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation; employ drip irrigation beneath plastic or straw mulch to minimize soil splash onto lower foliage. Avoid field operations while leaves are wet from morning dew or rainfall, as equipment and hands readily spread bacteria. Implement a minimum 2-year rotation with non-solanaceous crops such as maize, legumes, or cucurbits.

## Organic and Preventive Management

Apply preventive sprays of copper hydroxide or copper oxychloride (2.5 g per litre) combined with *Bacillus subtilis* or *Streptomyces* bio-formulations. Copper resistance in *Xanthomonas* is widespread, so combining copper with mancozeb enhances bactericidal copper ion efficacy through synergistic activity.

## Chemical Management

Where bacterial spot is confirmed during early vegetative or flowering stages, spray copper hydroxide (2 g per litre) mixed with mancozeb 75% WP (2 g per litre) at 7- to 10-day intervals during wet periods. Streptomycin sulfate (100 to 200 ppm) may be permitted in seedbed nurseries prior to transplanting under strict agricultural advisory. For Cercospora fungal spot, azoxystrobin (1 ml per litre) or difenoconazole (0.5 ml per litre) provides curative protection. Always follow local label guidelines and consult your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra.
