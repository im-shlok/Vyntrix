# Tomato Early Blight Leaf Management

Tomato early blight leaf disease is caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*. It is the most common
foliar disease of tomato in Indian conditions and is favoured by warm, humid
weather with alternating wet and dry periods.

## Identification

Lesions appear first on the oldest, lowest leaves as small dark brown spots
that enlarge into characteristic concentric rings, giving a target-board
appearance. A yellow halo usually surrounds each lesion. Severely affected
leaves turn yellow, dry up, and drop, exposing fruit to sunscald. Dark sunken
lesions with concentric rings may also form on the stem end of fruit.

Early blight is frequently confused with late blight. Late blight lesions are
irregular, water-soaked, and greyish-green with white fungal growth on the leaf
underside in humid weather, and it spreads far more rapidly in cool, wet
conditions.

## Cultural control

Maintain spacing of 60 x 45 cm to allow airflow through the canopy. Prune and
remove the lower leaves once lesions appear, and destroy infected debris rather
than composting it on the field. Avoid overhead irrigation, which splashes
spores onto healthy foliage; drip irrigation applied at the root zone keeps
leaves dry. Stake plants so that no foliage touches the soil. Practise a
minimum two-year rotation away from tomato, potato, brinjal, and chilli, all of
which host the same pathogen.

## Organic management

Neem oil applied at 3 to 5 ml per litre of water at the first appearance of
symptoms, repeated every 7 to 10 days, suppresses early infection. Spraying
*Trichoderma viride* or *Bacillus subtilis* based biocontrol formulations as a
preventive measure is effective where disease pressure is historically high.
Both options work prophylactically and will not cure advanced infection.

## Chemical management

Where more than roughly 10 percent of foliage is affected, copper oxychloride
at 3 g per litre or mancozeb 75% WP at 2 to 2.5 g per litre gives protective
control. For established infection, chlorothalonil or a difenoconazole-based
systemic fungicide is more effective. Rotate between fungicide groups to avoid
resistance and never apply the same active ingredient more than twice
consecutively.

Always follow the dose, pre-harvest interval, and protective equipment stated
on the product label, and confirm the choice with your local Krishi Vigyan
Kendra or agricultural officer before spraying. Do not spray when rain is
forecast within six hours or during peak afternoon heat.
