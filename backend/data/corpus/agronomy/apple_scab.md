# Apple Scab Leaf (Venturia inaequalis) Management

Apple scab is caused by the ascomycete fungus *Venturia inaequalis*. It is one of the most economically damaging diseases of apple orchards worldwide, thriving in cool, humid spring weather with prolonged leaf wetness.

## Identification

Early symptoms on apple leaves appear as olive-green to dull brown, velvety spots with indistinct margins, usually on the underside of young leaves as they unfold. As lesions mature, they become dark olive, circular, and velvety with distinct borders, eventually turning dark brown to black and corky. Heavily infected leaves become distorted, puckered, yellowed, and drop prematurely, causing severe defoliation that weakens trees and reduces fruit set.

## Distinguishing it from other apple leaf disorders

Cedar apple rust produces bright yellow-orange lesions with red borders and small black dots (pycnia) on the upper surface, unlike the dull olive-brown velvety lesions of apple scab. Apple powdery mildew forms white powdery patches on both leaf surfaces rather than olive-green spots. Frogeye leaf spot forms circular brown spots with purple margins and lighter centers.

## Cultural control

Orchard sanitation is critical because the fungus overwinters in fallen infected leaves on the orchard floor. Shred or sweep and compost fallen leaves after autumn leaf drop, or apply a 5% urea spray to fallen foliage to accelerate leaf breakdown before winter. Prune trees during dormancy to open the canopy, improving air circulation and sunlight penetration so leaves dry quickly after morning dew or rain. Plant resistant cultivars such as Prima, Priscilla, Enterprise, or Freedom when establishing new blocks.

## Organic management

Preventive sprays of wettable sulfur (3 to 5 g per litre of water) or copper-based fungicides such as copper oxychloride (2.5 to 3 g per litre) can be applied from silver tip through petal fall. Potassium bicarbonate sprays (3 g per litre) offer contact suppression during active sporulation. Repeat applications according to infection period tables based on temperature and hours of leaf wetness.

## Chemical management

At green tip through tight cluster stages, apply protective contact fungicides such as mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g per litre) or captan 50% WP (2 g per litre). During high-risk blossom and petal fall periods, systemic fungicides like difenoconazole (0.3 ml per litre), myclobutanil, or kresoxim-methyl provide curative and protective activity. Rotate fungicide chemical groups (FRAC codes) to prevent resistance development. Always respect pre-harvest intervals and consult local agricultural extension officers or the nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra for regional spray timing.
