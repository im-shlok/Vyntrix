# Corn Gray Leaf Spot (Cercospora zeae-maydis) Management

Gray leaf spot of corn (maize) is caused by the fungal pathogen *Cercospora zeae-maydis*. It is one of the most yield-limiting foliar diseases of corn worldwide, favoured by continuous corn cropping, minimum tillage, warm temperatures (25°C to 32°C), and extended periods of relative humidity above 90%.

## Identification

Early symptoms begin on the lower leaves as small, tan to brown spots with chlorotic yellow borders. As the fungus colonizes the leaf tissue, lesions expand parallel to the leaf veins, becoming distinct, blocky, strictly rectangular lesions bounded by the veins. Mature lesions are 2 to 7 cm long, rectangular, and turn silvery-gray or tan when producing spores. Under severe epidemics, thousands of lesions coalesce, causing extensive blighting of entire leaves, premature canopy death, and subsequent stalk rot and lodging.

## Distinguishing it from other corn foliar diseases

Gray leaf spot lesions are strictly rectangular with sharp right-angled ends bounded by parallel leaf veins. In contrast, Northern corn leaf blight produces large, elongated, elliptical or cigar-shaped lesions (3 to 15 cm long) with rounded or tapered ends. Common rust produces small, oval, powdery cinnamon-brown pustules that rupture the leaf epidermis.

## Cultural control

Because *Cercospora zeae-maydis* overwinters strictly in corn debris on the soil surface, residue management and crop rotation are the primary cultural defenses. Rotate fields out of corn for at least one to two years into non-host crops such as soybean, pulses, or cotton. In high-risk fields with minimum tillage, incorporate residue where erosion guidelines permit, or plant corn hybrids with high polygenic resistance ratings.

## Balanced Nutrition and Plant Density

Avoid excessive plant populations that create dense, humid microclimates within the canopy. Maintain balanced nitrogen-to-potassium ratios; potassium deficiency impairs vascular transport and significantly accelerates the severity of gray leaf spot.

## Chemical management

Fungicide application is recommended when high disease pressure threatens the ear leaf and leaves above the ear during the critical tasseling to silking stages (VT to R1). Effective fungicides include strobilurin and triazole premixes, such as azoxystrobin + difenoconazole (1 ml per litre), pyraclostrobin + fluxapyroxad, or propiconazole (1 ml per litre). Spraying after blister stage (R2) or when disease severity has already destroyed more than 50% of the upper canopy is generally not cost-effective. Always consult your regional Krishi Vigyan Kendra or agricultural extension specialist for local economic threshold advisories.
