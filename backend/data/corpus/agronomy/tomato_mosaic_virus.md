# Tomato Leaf Mosaic Virus (ToMV) Management

Tomato mosaic virus (ToMV) and the closely related Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV) are rod-shaped tobamoviruses capable of causing severe foliar malformation, stunting, and yield loss in tomato crops. Unlike insect-vectored viruses, tobamoviruses are exceptionally stable, highly contagious, and transmitted mechanically through simple contact.

## Identification

Infected tomato leaves exhibit a mottled pattern of alternating light green and dark green patches, creating a distinct "mosaic" appearance. In young plants, leaflets frequently exhibit blistering, puckering, rugosity, and downward curling. In severe strains, leaves undergo extreme distortion into narrow, thread-like or "shoestring" strips (fern-leaf symptom). Plant growth is stunted, especially if infection occurs early in the vegetative cycle. Fruit from infected plants often display internal browning of the fruit wall (vascular necrosis) and irregular blotchy ripening.

## Distinguishing Mosaic from Nutrient Deficiency and 2,4-D Herbicide Injury

Mosaic symptoms produce irregular light/dark green mottling without sharp boundaries. Iron or magnesium deficiency causes neat interveinal chlorosis with veins remaining strictly green. 2,4-D herbicide drift causes parallel strap-like vein elongation and downward cupping, but lacks the patchy green mosaic coloration.

## Transmission and Contagion Mechanics

ToMV is not transmitted by aphids or whiteflies. It is transmitted mechanically through contaminated hands, tools, clothing, machinery, and plant-to-plant contact. Workers who consume tobacco products can carry infectious tobamovirus particles on their hands for hours. The virus can persist for years on dry plant debris, seed coats, stakes, nursery flats, and greenhouse structures.

## Cultural and Hygiene Management

Hygiene is the cornerstone of tobamovirus management:
1. Plant resistant tomato hybrids carrying the *Tm-2* or *Tm-2^2* resistance genes.
2. Seed sanitation: Treat seeds with a 10% trisodium phosphate (TSP) soak for 30 minutes, or hot water treatment (50°C for 25 minutes) to inactivate external seed coat virus particles.
3. Strict handwashing: Mandate that field workers wash hands thoroughly with soap or 20% non-fat dry milk solution before working in tomato crops. Forbid smoking or handling tobacco products in or near tomato plots.
4. Tool disinfection: Sanitize pruning shears, trellising twine, and knives between plants using a 10% bleach solution or 20% powdered skim milk solution (the milk proteins bind and neutralize virus capsids).
5. Immediate roguing: Carefully pull up, bag, and burn any symptomatic plant as soon as symptoms emerge; do not drag infected plants across the field.

## Chemical Management

There are no chemical viricides capable of curing a plant infected with Tomato Mosaic Virus. Do not apply synthetic fungicides or insecticides to treat mosaic symptoms; focus exclusively on hygiene and resistant seed selection.
