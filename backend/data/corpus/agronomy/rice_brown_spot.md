# Rice Brown Spot and Leaf Discolouration

Brown spot of rice is caused by *Bipolaris oryzae* (formerly *Helminthosporium
oryzae*). Unlike most rice diseases it is primarily a symptom of poor soil
fertility and stress rather than of high inoculum pressure, so nutrition
correction matters more than fungicide.

## Identification

Lesions are small, circular to oval, dark brown spots scattered evenly across
the leaf blade, each typically 2 to 5 mm across with a grey or light brown
centre and a reddish-brown margin. Unlike blast lesions they are not spindle
shaped and do not have pointed ends. Severe infection on the panicle causes
grain discolouration and poor filling.

Brown spot is strongly associated with potassium and silicon deficiency,
drought stress, and unpuddled or sandy soils with poor water retention.

## Distinguishing it from other causes of browning

Bacterial leaf blight produces yellowing that begins at the leaf tip and
advances along the margin in wavy, water-soaked streaks. Rice blast produces
spindle-shaped lesions with grey centres and is far more aggressive. Simple
potassium deficiency without fungal involvement produces marginal yellowing and
scorching of older leaves and no discrete spots. Zinc deficiency causes
interveinal chlorosis on young leaves with stunted growth.

## Management

Correct the underlying nutrition first. Apply muriate of potash at 30 to 40 kg
per acre in split doses, and apply nitrogen in three splits rather than as a
single basal dose. Where soil tests confirm deficiency, a silicon amendment
through rice husk ash or calcium silicate measurably improves resistance.
Maintain a consistent 5 cm water level during tillering, since fluctuating
moisture stress predisposes the crop to infection.

Use certified seed from a regional agricultural university and treat seed with
carbendazim at 2 g per kg of seed, or with hot water at 53 to 54 degrees
Celsius for 10 to 12 minutes, before sowing. Destroy infected stubble after
harvest instead of incorporating it.

Where severity is high during grain filling, a foliar spray of hexaconazole 5%
EC at 2 ml per litre or propiconazole at 1 ml per litre is effective. Observe
the label pre-harvest interval and consult the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra
before applying any fungicide at the panicle stage.
