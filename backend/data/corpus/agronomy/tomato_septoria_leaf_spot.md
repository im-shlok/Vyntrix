# Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot Management

Septoria leaf spot of tomato is caused by the destructive fungal pathogen *Septoria lycopersici*. It is one of the most common and damaging foliar diseases of field and garden tomatoes, flourishing in warm (20°C to 26°C), wet, and humid conditions with frequent rains or prolonged overhead watering.

## Identification

Symptoms appear first on the lower leaves nearest the ground, usually after the first fruit clusters have set. Lesions are numerous, small, circular, and measure 2 to 4 mm in diameter. Each spot has a dark brown or black margin with a distinctive pale white, tan, or grey papery centre. As lesions mature, tiny, raised, black pepper-like specks (pycnidia, the fungal fruiting bodies) become clearly visible inside the pale centre of each spot under a hand lens. Affected leaves turn yellow, shrivel, curl, and drop off. Infection rapidly progresses upward from the ground level, often defoliating the entire plant except for a tuft of green foliage at the very top, leading to severe fruit sunscald.

## Distinguishing Septoria from Early Blight and Bacterial Spot

Septoria spots are much smaller (2 to 4 mm) and more numerous than early blight lesions (which are 10 to 15 mm with broad concentric rings). Septoria spots contain distinct black pinhead-sized pycnidia in their pale centres, which are completely absent in early blight and bacterial spot. Bacterial spot lesions are greasy, water-soaked, and have jagged or angular margins rather than neat circular borders.

## Cultural control

Because *Septoria lycopersici* overwinters in solanaceous crop debris and weed hosts (such as horsenettle and black nightshade), sanitation is the primary defense. Plough under or burn all tomato vines and debris immediately following harvest. Implement a strict minimum 3-year crop rotation away from solanaceous plants (tomato, potato, eggplant, peppers). Stake and trellis tomato plants to keep foliage elevated away from the ground. Apply a thick layer (5 to 8 cm) of clean straw or plastic mulch around the base of the plant to create a physical barrier against rain splash. Prune away the lowest 30 cm of leaves once the plant reaches 1 metre in height to eliminate the primary splash zone.

## Water Management

Never use overhead sprinkler irrigation; always apply water via drip irrigation lines at the root zone. Avoid working in or harvesting tomato fields when foliage is wet from rain or dew to prevent mechanically spreading sticky spores across the canopy.

## Chemical management

Preventive fungicide applications are critical once lower leaves show initial spotting. Apply protective contact fungicides such as chlorothalonil (2 g per litre), mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g per litre), or copper oxychloride (3 g per litre) on a 7- to 10-day schedule during wet spells. In severe outbreaks, systemic fungicides such as azoxystrobin (1 ml per litre), pyraclostrobin, or difenoconazole (0.5 ml per litre) provide translaminar protection.
