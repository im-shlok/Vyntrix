# Apple Rust Leaf (Cedar Apple Rust) Management

Cedar apple rust is caused by the heteroecious rust fungus *Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae*. It requires two distinct host species to complete its complex two-year life cycle: apple or crabapple (*Malus* spp.) and Eastern red cedar or juniper (*Juniperus* spp.).

## Identification

In early spring, small pale yellow spots appear on the upper surface of apple leaves shortly after bloom. These spots enlarge, turning bright yellow-orange to fiery reddish-orange with a bright yellow border. Tiny black specks (pycnia) develop in the centre of these lesions, exuding sweet droplets that attract insects. Several weeks later, cylindrical tube-like structures or projections (aecia) appear on the lower leaf surface directly beneath the spots. Heavily infected leaves become brittle, curl, drop prematurely, and leave trees vulnerable to secondary stresses.

## Distinguishing it from other apple leaf spots

Apple rust lesions are vivid bright orange with prominent yellow borders and lower-surface fungal tubes, unlike apple scab which produces dull velvety olive-green spots without tube-like projections. Alternaria blotch lesions are brownish with black concentric rings, while frogeye leaf spot lesions are tan with purple borders.

## Cultural and Landscape Management

Where feasible, eradicate cedar and juniper bushes within a radius of 500 metres to 1 kilometre around the apple orchard to break the pathogen's heteroecious infection cycle. During winter or early spring, prune and destroy the brown, woody galls ("cedar apples") on nearby junipers before they produce orange gelatinous telial spore horns during warm spring rains. Plant rust-immune or resistant apple varieties such as Redfree, Liberty, Freedom, or Enterprise in areas with heavy wild juniper presence.

## Organic and Preventive Management

Apply preventive copper-based bactericides or wettable sulfur (3 to 5 g per litre) starting at pink bud stage through petal fall. Applications must precede wetting events since infection occurs within 4 to 6 hours of leaf wetness when temperatures are between 12°C and 24°C.

## Chemical Management

Protecting apple foliage during the critical release period (from pink bud stage until 2 to 3 weeks after petal fall) is paramount. Effective fungicides include myclobutanil (0.4 g per litre), tebuconazole, mancozeb 75% WP (2 g per litre), or trifloxystrobin. Fungicide applications after aecia have formed on leaf undersides are ineffective for that growing season. Consult your regional horticulture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra for local teliospore release alerts.
