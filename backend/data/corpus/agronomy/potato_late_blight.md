# Potato Leaf Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) Management

Potato late blight is caused by the oomycete pathogen *Phytophthora infestans*. It is historically the most devastating disease of potato crops, notorious for causing total crop loss within days under cool (12°C to 20°C), continuously damp weather with relative humidity above 90%.

## Identification

Late blight appears initially as water-soaked, irregular, pale to dark green lesions at leaf tips or margins. Under humid conditions, lesions rapidly expand into large, dark brown to purplish-black necrotic blotches that cross major leaf veins without restriction. The most definitive diagnostic symptom is a delicate, white, cottony fungal-like growth (sporangiophores and sporangia) visible on the underside of the leaf along the active margin between infected and healthy tissue, particularly in early morning humidity or after rain. Infected foliage quickly turns black, wilts, and gives off a characteristic pungent odor of rotting potato vegetation.

## Distinguishing Late Blight from Early Blight

Late blight produces rapidly expanding, irregular, water-soaked, dark lesions with white mold on the underside during humid periods. Early blight produces dry, circular, leathery brown spots with concentric target-board rings and yellow halos, strictly without white fungal down on the lower leaf surface.

## Cultural control

Plant certified disease-free seed tubers obtained from reputable government or seed corporation sources. Choose resistant varieties (such as Kufri Girdhari, Kufri Himalini, or Kufri Mohan in India). Eliminate cull piles, volunteer potato plants, and solanaceous weeds around field perimeters, as these serve as the primary reservoir for overwintering *Phytophthora* mycelium. Ensure wide ridge spacing (60 to 75 cm) and earthing up to create a 10 to 15 cm soil buffer over developing tubers, preventing washing of spores into the soil bed.

## Forecasting and Chemical Management

Because late blight moves with explosive speed, preventive protection is essential. Monitor regional late blight decision support systems or Blightwatch advisories. Apply protective contact fungicides such as mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g per litre), chlorothalonil (2 g per litre), or copper oxychloride (3 g per litre) prior to rain spells or dense fogs. 

If active blight symptoms are detected in the area, switch to translaminar or systemic anti-oomycete compounds: cymoxanil + mancozeb (2.5 g per litre), dimethomorph (1.5 g per litre), metalaxyl-M + mancozeb (2.5 g per litre), or mandipropamid (1 ml per litre). Do not use single-site systemic fungicides consecutively. Kill haulms with a desiccant or cutting 10 to 14 days before harvest to prevent tuber contamination. Consult your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra immediately upon symptom appearance.
