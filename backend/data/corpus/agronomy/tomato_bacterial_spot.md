# Tomato Leaf Bacterial Spot Management

Bacterial spot of tomato is caused by four species of plant-pathogenic bacteria: *Xanthomonas vesicatoria*, *Xanthomonas euvesicatoria*, *Xanthomonas perforans*, and *Xanthomonas gardneri*. The disease causes substantial crop injury in warm (24°C to 30°C), humid, rainy climates, producing severe foliar defoliation, blossom drop, and unmarketable scabby fruit lesions.

## Identification

Foliar lesions appear as small, water-soaked, circular to irregular or angular spots measuring 2 to 3 mm across, primarily on the lower leaf surface initially. On the upper leaf surface, the lesions turn dark brown to black and develop a distinctive greasy, water-soaked appearance often surrounded by a narrow yellow halo. Unlike fungal leaf spots, the centres of bacterial spot lesions frequently tear and fall out during dry, windy periods, giving the foliage a ragged, "shot-hole" appearance. Severe epidemics lead to general yellowing, leaf necrosis, and widespread defoliation, exposing green fruit to direct solar radiation and sunscald.

## Distinguishing Bacterial Spot from Fungal Spots

Bacterial spot lesions are greasy or water-soaked when held to the light and lack the concentric target rings of *Alternaria* (early blight) and the pepper-like black pycnidia of *Septoria*. When a fresh lesion is cut and mounted in water under a compound microscope, streaming clouds of bacterial cells (*bacterial streaming*) can be observed emerging from the vascular tissue within minutes.

## Cultural control

Plant only certified, pathogen-free seeds tested for *Xanthomonas*. Perform hot water seed treatment (50°C for 25 minutes) to eliminate internal and external seed-borne bacteria. Avoid handling plants or cultivating fields while leaves are wet. Use drip irrigation under black plastic mulch to eliminate splashing water. Disinfect stakes, pruning shears, and nursery trays with a 10% household bleach or quaternary ammonium solution between plantings. Implement a minimum 2-year crop rotation out of solanaceous crops into corn, beans, or sorghum.

## Organic and Preventive Biocontrol

Foliar sprays of *Bacillus subtilis* or *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* formulations act as competitive antagonists against *Xanthomonas* colonization. Bacteriophage treatments (formulations containing specific bacterial viruses) applied preventatively before evening rain events effectively reduce bacterial populations without risk of chemical resistance.

## Chemical management

Traditional control relies on preventive sprays of copper fungicides (copper hydroxide or copper oxychloride at 2.5 g per litre) combined with mancozeb 75% WP (2 g per litre). The addition of mancozeb releases more active copper ions and helps overcome widespread copper resistance in field *Xanthomonas* populations. In severe nursery outbreaks, agricultural streptomycin sprays (100 to 200 ppm) may be applied under state advisory prior to transplanting. Always consult your regional Krishi Vigyan Kendra for local bacterial resistance alerts.
