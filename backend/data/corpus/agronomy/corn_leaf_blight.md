# Corn Leaf Blight (Northern Corn Leaf Blight) Management

Northern corn leaf blight (NCLB) is caused by the fungus *Exserohilum turcicum* (syn. *Setosphaeria turcica*). It affects field corn, sweet corn, and popcorn, thriving in moderate temperatures (18°C to 27°C) accompanied by frequent rains, heavy morning dews, and high relative humidity.

## Identification

The hallmark symptom of Northern corn leaf blight is the presence of large, elliptical, cigar-shaped or spindle-shaped lesions on the leaves. Early lesions appear as small, water-soaked, grayish-green streaks that expand rapidly along the leaf blade into long lesions measuring 3 to 15 cm in length and 1 to 3 cm in width. As lesions mature, they become tan to grayish-brown with rounded or tapered ends. During damp mornings, dark olive-black fungal spores form velvety layers inside the centres of the lesions. Severe infection causes extensive foliar necrosis, burning look to the canopy, poor ear filling, and chaffy kernels.

## Distinguishing it from Gray Leaf Spot and Southern Blight

NCLB lesions are distinctly elliptical or cigar-shaped with smooth rounded or tapered ends that cut across leaf veins. Gray leaf spot lesions are strictly rectangular, blocky, and bounded by leaf veins. Southern corn leaf blight produces much smaller (1 to 3 cm), narrower, parallel-sided lesions with reddish-brown borders.

## Cultural control

Plant resistant hybrids carrying specific single-gene resistance (*Ht1*, *Ht2*, *Ht3*, or *HtN*) or high multi-genic quantitative resistance. Rotate corn with non-grass crops such as soybean, sunflower, or legumes for at least one full season to reduce the primary inoculum surviving in corn stubble. Shred and incorporate infected residue post-harvest where appropriate to accelerate decomposition.

## Plant Population and Irrigation Timing

Avoid overly dense seeding rates that reduce air movement within the lower corn canopy. In irrigated corn systems, avoid late afternoon or evening overhead irrigation cycles that prolong canopy leaf wetness throughout the night. Schedule watering for early morning hours so leaves dry rapidly under the sun.

## Chemical management

Scouting from V8 stage through tasseling (VT) is vital. If lesions appear on lower leaves prior to tasseling and weather forecasts predict continuous wet conditions, an application of a triazole or strobilurin fungicide is justified. Recommended formulations include azoxystrobin (1 ml per litre), tebuconazole (1 ml per litre), mancozeb 75% WP (2 to 2.5 g per litre), or fluxapyroxad + pyraclostrobin. Direct sprays toward the canopy middle to protect the ear leaf (the primary contributor to ear grain fill). Consult local agricultural university extension guidelines or the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra before application.
