# Corn Rust Leaf (Common Rust - Puccinia sorghi) Management

Common rust of corn (maize) is caused by the obligate biotrophic fungus *Puccinia sorghi*. Unlike many other foliar pathogens, *Puccinia sorghi* does not overwinter in crop residue in temperate regions; instead, lightweight urediniospores are carried over long distances on prevailing southerly winds and storm fronts each growing season.

## Identification

Common rust produces small, oval to elongate, cinnamon-brown to golden-brown pustules (uredinia) scattered across both the upper and lower surfaces of corn leaves. As the pustules mature, the corn leaf epidermis ruptures, revealing powdery reddish-brown masses of fungal spores that rub off on fingers or clothing. Later in the season or as temperatures rise, the pustules turn dark brownish-black as teliospores form. When infection is severe, pustules coalesce, chlorotic yellow bands envelope the leaf blade, and leaves tear, shred, and dry up prematurely.

## Distinguishing Common Rust from Southern Corn Rust

Common rust pustules appear with equal abundance on both the upper and lower leaf surfaces, are dark cinnamon-brown, and rupture the epidermis readily. Southern rust (*Puccinia polysora*) pustules are smaller, circular, densely crowded almost exclusively on the upper leaf surface, light orange to golden-brown, and remain covered by the epidermis for a prolonged period. Common rust prefers cool to moderate temperatures (16°C to 25°C), whereas Southern rust thrives in tropical heat (above 27°C).

## Cultural and Agronomic Management

Planting corn hybrids carrying the dominant *Rp1-D* gene or robust general polygenic resistance provides the most cost-effective protection. In regions with known annual rust pressure, plant early in the season so that the crop passes the critical pollination and grain-filling periods before airborne spore loads peak. Ensure balanced soil nutrition: crops with excessive nitrogen fertilization and deficient potassium are markedly more susceptible to severe rust colonization.

## Organic Management

In organic production or sweet corn patches, early preventative sprays of wettable sulfur (3 g per litre) or potassium silicate formulations provide surface barrier protection against spore germination. Biocontrol agents such as *Bacillus subtilis* or *Trichoderma harzianum* applied prophylactically reduce pustule count.

## Chemical Management

Fungicide treatment is rarely needed on field corn unless a susceptible hybrid is planted and pustules appear on leaves below or at the ear leaf prior to tasseling (VT). On seed production fields or sweet corn, fungicides are frequently economic. Effective active ingredients include azoxystrobin + propiconazole (1 ml per litre), tebuconazole (1 ml per litre), pyraclostrobin, or mancozeb 75% WP (2 g per litre). Ensure complete canopy coverage. Consult your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra for regional spore-trapping alerts and treatment thresholds.
