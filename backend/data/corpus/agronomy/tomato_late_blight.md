# Tomato Leaf Late Blight Management

Late blight of tomato is caused by the oomycete microorganism *Phytophthora infestans*. It is one of the most explosive and catastrophic plant diseases known, capable of turning healthy tomato fields into blackened, collapsed ruins within a few days when temperatures hover between 12°C and 22°C and relative humidity exceeds 90%.

## Identification

Early symptoms show up as irregular, water-soaked, grayish-green or olive-green lesions on the leaves, petioles, and green stems. Lesions expand with incredible speed during cool, wet periods, darkening into large, purplish-brown to greasy-black necrotic blotches that do not respect leaf veins. The crucial diagnostic sign appears on the underside of infected leaves during damp mornings or after rain: a delicate, white, velvety or downy fungal-like bloom of sporangia bordering the advancing edge of the necrotic lesion. Petioles and stems develop large, dark brown, greasy girdling lesions, causing the upper canopy to wilt and topple. A distinct, foul odor of rotting solanaceous tissue fills the infected field.

## Distinguishing Late Blight from Early Blight and Septoria

Late blight expands rapidly, crossing leaf veins and creating dark water-soaked blotches with white downy fungal growth on the leaf underside in moist conditions. Early blight lesions are dry, brown, circular, restricted by veins, and possess concentric "target-board" rings without white mold. Septoria spots are tiny (2 to 4 mm) with pale gray centres containing black pinhead pycnidia.

## Cultural control

Plant certified disease-free transplants. Choose resistant or tolerant tomato varieties (such as those containing *Ph-2* and *Ph-3* resistance genes, e.g., Mountain Magic, Defiant, or Iron Lady). Avoid overhead irrigation; use drip lines at ground level. Destroy volunteer potato and tomato plants and eliminate wild solanaceous weeds around field borders. Space plants adequately (60 x 60 cm minimum) to facilitate rapid canopy drying. Immediately rogue out and bag infected plants at first discovery to arrest spore dissemination across neighbouring fields.

## Chemical management

Preventive sprays are paramount when regional late blight advisories trigger. Apply protective contact fungicides such as mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g per litre), chlorothalonil (2 g per litre), or copper hydroxide (2.5 g per litre) prior to prolonged rain or fog events. 

When active infection is confirmed nearby, immediately apply systemic or translaminar anti-oomycete fungicides: cymoxanil + mancozeb (2.5 g per litre), dimethomorph (1.5 g per litre), metalaxyl-M + mancozeb (2.5 g per litre), or mandipropamid (1 ml per litre). Alternate chemical classes (FRAC codes) to prevent resistance. Adhere strictly to the pre-harvest intervals and consult your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra immediately upon suspecting late blight.
