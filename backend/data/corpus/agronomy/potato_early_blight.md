# Potato Leaf Early Blight (Alternaria solani) Management

Potato early blight is caused by the necrotrophic fungal pathogen *Alternaria solani*. Despite its name, early blight typically becomes prominent mid-to-late in the potato growing season as the crop sets and bulks tubers, accelerating under high humidity and warm temperatures (24°C to 30°C) with alternating wet and dry foliage periods.

## Identification

The earliest symptoms appear on older, lower foliage as small, circular, dark brown to black spots. As these spots enlarge (up to 1.5 cm across), they develop distinctive concentric rings that create a characteristic "target-board" or "bullseye" appearance. A distinct chlorotic yellow halo frequently borders each lesion. Infected leaves eventually turn completely yellow, wither, roll upwards, and collapse, while remaining attached to the potato stem. In heavy infestations, premature canopy defoliation severely curtails tuber sizing and yield.

## Distinguishing Early Blight from Late Blight

Early blight produces dry, leathery, dark brown lesions with defined concentric rings and a yellow halo, restricted by larger leaf veins. Late blight (*Phytophthora infestans*) produces irregular, water-soaked, greasy, dark green to purplish-black lesions that spread rapidly across veins with pale green borders, producing a delicate white fungal mold on the underside of leaves during damp mornings.

## Cultural control

Use certified disease-free seed tubers and plant varieties with moderate field resistance (such as Kufri Jyoti or Kufri Pukhraj where adapted). Practise a strict three-year crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops such as maize, wheat, mustard, or sugarcane to starve surviving soil inoculum. Ensure balanced crop nutrition: plants experiencing nitrogen deficiency, drought stress, or early senescence are substantially more vulnerable to early blight. Maintain adequate potassium fertilization (100 to 120 kg/ha K2O) to strengthen foliar cell walls.

## Irrigation and Residue Sanitation

Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation late in the afternoon that keeps leaves damp overnight. Apply water through furrows or drip lines. Remove and destroy infected haulms and vines after harvest, or plough them deeply beneath the soil surface to hasten residue breakdown.

## Chemical management

Preventive fungicide applications should commence before canopy closure when lower foliage begins to lodge and ground moisture stays high. Protective contact fungicides include mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g per litre), chlorothalonil (2 g per litre), or propineb (2.5 g per litre). When active lesions appear and weather favours disease spread, rotate to systemic compounds such as difenoconazole (0.5 ml per litre), azoxystrobin (1 ml per litre), or pyraclostrobin + metiram. Alternate fungicide modes of action (FRAC codes) to suppress fungicide resistance. Always confirm application timing with your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra.
