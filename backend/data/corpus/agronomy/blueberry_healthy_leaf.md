# Healthy Blueberry Leaf Maintenance and Acidic Soil Management

A healthy blueberry (*Vaccinium corymbosum* / *Vaccinium ashei*) leaf is ovate to lanceolate, glossy, leather-textured, and rich dark green, often turning brilliant scarlet or crimson in autumn. Blueberry bushes possess unique physiological requirements, notably an obligate demand for acidic soil and ammonium-based nitrogen.

## Soil Acidity (pH) and Foliage Coloration

Blueberries lack root hairs and rely heavily on symbiotic ericoid mycorrhizae that require acidic soils with a pH between 4.5 and 5.2. When soil pH exceeds 5.5, blueberry leaves quickly manifest interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between green veins) on young terminal leaves due to iron and manganese lockout. Incorporate elemental sulfur or peat moss into planting beds 6 to 12 months prior to planting to adjust soil pH to the optimal range. Apply chelated iron (Fe-EDDHA) at 5 to 10 g per bush if early interveinal yellowing is observed.

## Nutrient Requirements

Never apply nitrate forms of nitrogen (such as calcium nitrate or potassium nitrate), as nitrate ions induce chlorosis and plant decline. Use ammonium sulfate ((NH4)2SO4) or urea as the primary nitrogen source, applied in three split applications: at bud break, six weeks later, and post-harvest. Ensure moderate phosphorus and potassium (potassium sulfate, not potassium chloride, as blueberries are sensitive to chloride toxicity).

## Moisture and Pine Bark Mulch

Maintain a 7 to 10 cm layer of aged pine bark mulch around the root zone. Blueberry root systems are fibrous, shallow (top 30 cm of soil), and highly susceptible to both drought desiccation and hypoxic root rot from saturation. Maintain uniform drip irrigation delivering 25 to 40 mm equivalent weekly.

## Plant Health and Monitoring

Regularly inspect foliage for blueberry gall midge, thrips, and early signs of anthracnose or mummy berry (*Monilinia vaccinii-corymbosi*). A healthy blueberry canopy with vibrant leaves requires no routine synthetic pesticides, supporting bumblebee and solitary bee pollination critical for high fruit set.
