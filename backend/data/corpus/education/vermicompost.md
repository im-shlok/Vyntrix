# Making Vermicompost on the Farm

## Summary
Vermicompost is a nutrient-rich organic manure made by earthworms feeding on crop residue and cow dung. A shaded bed of partly decomposed dung and residue, kept moist, gives ready compost in about two to three months. It improves soil structure and water holding, and cuts fertiliser costs.

## Steps
1. Choose a shaded, well-drained spot, and make a bed or pit about 1 metre wide and 30 to 60 cm deep, of any convenient length.
2. Lay a base of coarse material such as sugarcane trash or coconut husk for drainage.
3. Add layers of partly decomposed cow dung mixed with chopped crop residue and vegetable waste.
4. Release composting earthworms such as Eisenia fetida (red wigglers) onto the bed.
5. Keep the bed as moist as a squeezed sponge by sprinkling water regularly, and cover it with gunny bags.
6. When the material turns dark, crumbly and earthy-smelling, stop watering, heap it in the light so the worms move down, and harvest the compost from the top.

## Using vermicompost
Apply about 2 to 3 tonnes per acre for field crops, mixed into the soil at
sowing, or a handful to a cupful per plant for vegetables and fruit trees.

## Common mistakes
- Using fresh dung, which heats up and kills the worms
- Letting the bed dry out or become waterlogged
- Adding oily, salty or spicy food waste, plastic or glass
- Keeping the bed in direct sunlight
