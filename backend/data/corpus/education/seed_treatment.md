# Seed Treatment Before Sowing

## Summary
Seed treatment protects seeds and young seedlings from seed-borne and soil-borne diseases and early insect attack at very low cost. When using more than one product, follow the FIR order: Fungicide first, then Insecticide, then Rhizobium or another biofertiliser.

## Steps
1. Use certified seed, and check the bag first. Much certified seed is already treated and should not be treated again.
2. Follow the FIR order when using more than one product: Fungicide, then Insecticide, then Rhizobium or another biofertiliser.
3. For fungicide treatment, mix seed with the recommended dose, commonly 2 to 3 g per kg of seed for products such as carbendazim or thiram, or 4 to 10 g per kg for Trichoderma.
4. For pulses, coat the seed with Rhizobium culture mixed in a little jaggery solution just before sowing, and dry it in the shade.
5. Mix seed and product in a closed seed-treating drum or a plastic bag while wearing gloves; never mix by bare hand.
6. Sow treated seed the same day or as soon as possible, and never use leftover treated seed as food or animal feed.

## Why it matters
Wilt, root rot, damping off, smut and seedling blights often start from
infected seed or soil. Seed treatment costs only a few rupees per acre and can
prevent heavy losses at the seedling stage.
