# Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Basics

## Summary
Integrated Pest Management combines resistant varieties, good field practices, regular scouting, traps and natural enemies, and uses chemical pesticides only when pest numbers cross the economic threshold level. It lowers costs, protects pollinators and natural enemies, and slows pesticide resistance.

## The IPM approach
1. Prevent: use healthy certified seed, resistant varieties, crop rotation, timely sowing and balanced fertiliser.
2. Monitor: walk the field once or twice a week in a zig-zag path, checking plants for pests, eggs and damage.
3. Trap: use pheromone traps to track moths such as pink bollworm and fruit borers, yellow sticky traps for whiteflies and aphids, and light traps for night-flying pests.
4. Protect natural enemies: ladybird beetles, spiders, lacewings and parasitic wasps kill many pests for free, and broad-spectrum sprays kill them too.
5. Use biological controls such as Trichoderma, Bacillus thuringiensis, NPV and neem-based products where they suit the pest.
6. Spray a recommended chemical only when the pest crosses its economic threshold level, at the label dose, rotating between chemical groups.

## Economic threshold level
The economic threshold level is the pest population at which the cost of
control becomes lower than the damage the pest would cause. Thresholds differ
by crop and pest; ask your Krishi Vigyan Kendra or agriculture officer for the
thresholds for your crop.
