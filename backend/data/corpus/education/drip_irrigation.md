# Drip Irrigation & Water Management

## Summary
Drip irrigation delivers water slowly to the root zone through pipes and emitters, saving 30% to 50% of water compared with flood irrigation while reducing weeds and disease. Place drippers close to the root zone, keep filters clean, and check soil moisture before each irrigation instead of watering on a fixed schedule.

## Parts of a drip system
- Pump and control valve
- Screen or disc filter, with an extra sand filter for canal or pond water
- Venturi injector or fertiliser tank for fertigation
- Main and sub-main pipes
- Lateral pipes with emitters along each plant row

## Running the system well
1. Irrigate daily or on alternate days for short periods instead of giving long, infrequent irrigations.
2. Check soil moisture with a tensiometer, or by feeling the soil at root depth, before irrigating.
3. Apply water-soluble fertilisers through the drip in small, frequent doses (fertigation).
4. Clean the filters regularly, and flush the ends of the lateral pipes every week or two.
5. Treat clogged emitters with acid or chlorine as advised by the system supplier.
6. Walk the lines while the system runs to find leaks and blocked drippers.

## Subsidy
Drip and sprinkler systems are subsidised under the Per Drop More Crop
component of PMKSY: 55% of the cost for small and marginal farmers and 45% for
other farmers.
