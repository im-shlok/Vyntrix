# How to treat yellow spots on Tomato leaves

## Summary
Yellow spots on tomato leaves usually point to a fungal disease such as early blight or septoria leaf spot, or to a nutrient deficiency. Identify the pattern first. Then keep soil moisture even, remove infected lower leaves, spray neem oil at the first signs, and use a fungicide such as mancozeb or copper only if the infection keeps spreading.

## Reading the pattern
- Brown spots with concentric rings and a yellow halo on older leaves point to early blight (Alternaria solani).
- Many small round spots with grey centres and dark borders point to septoria leaf spot.
- Yellowing between the veins of older leaves, while the veins stay green, points to magnesium deficiency.
- Even, pale yellowing of the oldest leaves points to nitrogen deficiency.
- Yellow, curled, crinkled young leaves on stunted plants point to a virus spread by whiteflies.

## What to do
1. Remove and destroy spotted lower leaves, and stake plants so foliage stays off the ground.
2. Water at the base of the plant or by drip, in the morning, so the leaves stay dry.
3. Spray neem oil at 3 to 5 ml per litre of water at the first sign of fungal spots.
4. If spots keep spreading, use a protective fungicide such as mancozeb or copper oxychloride, following the label.
5. Correct nutrient deficiencies based on a soil test rather than by guessing.
6. Control whiteflies early if leaves curl, because a virus cannot be cured once plants are infected.
