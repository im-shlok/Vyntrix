# How to Take a Soil Sample for Testing

## Summary
A soil test is only as good as the sample. Collect soil from 8 to 10 spots across a uniform field at plough depth, mix it well, and send about half a kilogram to the soil testing laboratory with your details, ideally after harvest and before applying fertiliser for the next crop.

## Steps
1. Sample each uniform field separately. Do not mix soil from fields that differ in colour, slope, crop or management.
2. Avoid spots near bunds, trees, manure heaps, irrigation channels or recently fertilised patches.
3. Scrape away surface litter, then dig a V-shaped cut 15 cm deep (plough depth) with a spade or khurpi.
4. Take a 2 cm thick slice from one side of the cut, from top to bottom. Repeat at 8 to 10 spots in a zig-zag pattern across the field.
5. Mix all the slices on a clean cloth or sheet, then reduce the mixture to about 500 g by quartering: divide it into four, discard two opposite quarters, and repeat.
6. Dry the sample in the shade, pack it in a clean cloth or plastic bag, and label it with your name, the field number, the previous crop and the crop to be grown.

## When to sample
Sample after harvest and before the next crop's fertiliser is applied, and
repeat every two to three years. Orchards are sampled at more than one depth;
follow the laboratory's instructions for those.
