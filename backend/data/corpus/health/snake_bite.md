# Snake Bite First Aid

Snakebite is a serious occupational hazard for Indian farmers, especially
during the monsoon, at sowing and harvest, when walking through fields at
night, and when sleeping on the floor. Most deaths in India are caused by the
"Big Four": the spectacled cobra, common krait, Russell's viper and saw-scaled
viper. The anti-snake venom (ASV) used in Indian hospitals is polyvalent and
covers all four, so the snake does not need to be caught or killed for
treatment.

The national snakebite protocol summarises first aid as "Do it R.I.G.H.T.":
Reassure the patient, Immobilise the bitten limb, Get to hospital immediately,
and Tell the doctor about any symptoms that develop on the way.

## Symptoms
- Pain, swelling or bruising spreading from the bite site, common with vipers
- Bleeding from the gums, nose or bite wound, or blood in the urine
- Drooping eyelids, double vision, or difficulty swallowing or speaking, common with cobra and krait bites
- Difficulty breathing, weakness or drowsiness
- Stomach pain or vomiting, which after a krait bite at night may be the only early sign because the bite itself is often painless

## First aid steps
1. Move away from the snake, keep the patient calm and still, and reassure them that the bite is treatable in hospital.
2. Remove rings, bangles, anklets, watches and tight clothing from the bitten limb before swelling starts.
3. Immobilise the bitten limb with a splint or folded cloth as you would a broken bone, without tying it tightly.
4. Do NOT cut the wound, suck out venom, apply a tight tourniquet, ice, herbs or electric shock.
5. Call 108 and carry the patient, lying on their side, to the nearest hospital or community health centre that stocks anti-snake venom.
6. Note the time of the bite and tell the doctor about every symptom that appears on the way, especially drooping eyelids or difficulty breathing.

## Do not
- Do not waste time on traditional healers, chanting or herbal remedies. Delay in reaching hospital is the main cause of snakebite deaths.
- Do not tie a tight band, rope or cloth above the bite. It does not stop venom and can cause gangrene.
- Do not give alcohol, or painkillers such as aspirin that can worsen bleeding.
- Do not let the patient walk or run if it can be avoided, since movement spreads venom faster.

## Prevention
- Use a torch when walking at night and wear boots or closed footwear in the field.
- Sleep on a raised cot under a tucked-in mosquito net. Kraits bite people sleeping on the floor.
- Keep grass, firewood and stored grain away from the house, since rodents attract snakes.
- Check bunds, haystacks and dense crop with a stick before putting hands or feet in.
