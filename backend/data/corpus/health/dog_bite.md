# Dog and Animal Bites: Rabies Prevention

Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms appear, but it is completely
preventable with prompt wound washing and vaccination. In India most cases
follow dog bites. Bites or scratches from cats, monkeys, mongooses, jackals
and other animals, and saliva from a rabid cow or buffalo on broken skin, also
carry risk. Treat every animal bite that breaks the skin as a possible rabies
exposure.

## First aid steps
1. Immediately wash the wound with soap and plenty of running water for at least 15 minutes. This alone removes much of the virus.
2. After washing, apply an antiseptic such as povidone-iodine if available.
3. Cover the wound loosely with a clean cloth. Do not burn the wound or bandage it tightly.
4. Go to the nearest government hospital or health centre the same day for anti-rabies vaccine, even if the wound looks small.
5. For a bite or scratch that draws blood, or saliva on broken skin, the mouth or the eyes, the doctor will also give rabies immunoglobulin into and around the wound.
6. Complete every dose of the vaccine schedule the doctor gives, and take a tetanus injection if advised.

## Do not
- Do not apply chilli, turmeric, oil, lime, mud or herbal pastes to the wound.
- Do not wait to see whether the animal falls ill before starting vaccination. Start immediately, and tell the doctor if the dog or cat is still healthy after 10 days.
- Do not stop the vaccine course early.

## Livestock and rabies
If a cow, buffalo, goat or dog on the farm shows sudden behaviour change,
aggression, heavy drooling, difficulty swallowing or paralysis, keep people
away from it and contact the veterinary officer. Anyone bitten by such an
animal, or whose broken skin or mouth was exposed to its saliva, needs
post-exposure vaccination.
