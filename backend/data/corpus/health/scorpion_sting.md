# Scorpion Sting First Aid

Scorpion stings are common in rural India, particularly in Maharashtra,
Gujarat, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Tamil Nadu.
Stings from the Indian red scorpion can be life-threatening, especially for
children, because the venom affects the heart and can cause fluid in the
lungs. Effective hospital treatment is available, so getting the person to
hospital early matters far more than any home remedy.

## Symptoms
- Severe burning pain at the sting site, sometimes spreading up the limb
- Sweating, cold hands and feet, vomiting or heavy drooling
- A fast or irregular heartbeat and restlessness
- Breathlessness, a frothy cough or a cold, clammy body, which signal an emergency

## First aid steps
1. Keep the person calm and still, and remove rings or tight items near the sting.
2. Wash the sting site with soap and water.
3. Apply a cold, wet cloth or wrapped ice to the site for 10 minutes at a time to ease the pain.
4. Take the person to the nearest hospital or health centre promptly, especially a child, an elderly person, or anyone sweating or vomiting.
5. Call 108 immediately if there is breathlessness, chest discomfort or a very fast heartbeat.

## Do not
- Do not cut the sting site or try to suck out venom.
- Do not tie a tight band above the sting.
- Do not rely on mantras, herbal pastes or magnets. They delay treatment.

## Prevention
- Shake out shoes, clothes and bedding before use, and wear footwear at night.
- Wear gloves when lifting stones, firewood, hay or stacked bricks.
- Seal cracks in walls and floors, and keep the area around the house clear of debris.
