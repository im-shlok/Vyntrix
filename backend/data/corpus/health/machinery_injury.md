# Farm Machinery Injuries: Bleeding and Amputation

Chaff cutters, threshers, tractors, rotavators and power tillers cause many
serious farm injuries in India, especially to hands and arms caught in moving
parts. Fast control of bleeding and quick transport to hospital save lives and
can save limbs.

## First aid steps
1. Stop the machine and switch off the engine or power supply before reaching in to help the injured person.
2. Press firmly on the bleeding wound with a clean cloth, and keep pressing without lifting it to check. Add more cloth on top if blood soaks through.
3. Keep the injured person lying down, still and warm.
4. If bleeding from an arm or leg is life-threatening and does not stop with firm pressure, tie a wide band tightly above the wound as a last resort, and note the time it was applied.
5. If a finger or part of a limb is cut off, wrap it in a clean damp cloth, seal it in a plastic bag, and keep the bag on ice or in cold water. Never put the part directly on ice.
6. Call 108 and take the person, and any amputated part, to hospital immediately.

## Do not
- Do not pull out an object stuck deep in a wound. Pad around it and press around the edges.
- Do not put soil, cow dung, ash or powders on a wound to stop bleeding.
- Do not give the injured person food or alcohol, as they may need surgery.

## Prevention
- Never feed fodder into a chaff cutter by hand close to the rollers. Use a pushing stick, and keep the safety guard fitted.
- Keep all belt, chain and shaft guards in place, and switch off before clearing a blockage.
- Do not wear loose clothing, dupattas or loose hair near rotating shafts, especially the tractor PTO shaft.
- Keep children away from working machinery, and never carry extra riders on a tractor.
