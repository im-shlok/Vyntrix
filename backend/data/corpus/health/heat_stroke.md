# Heat Stroke and Heat Exhaustion

Field work during Indian summers, especially in the pre-monsoon heatwave
months, puts farmers and labourers at risk of heat illness. Heat exhaustion is
the warning stage. Heat stroke is a medical emergency in which the body can no
longer cool itself; without fast cooling it damages the brain and other organs
and can kill.

## Symptoms
- Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps and a fast pulse
- Heat stroke: very high body temperature with hot, red skin that may be dry or still sweating
- Heat stroke: confusion, slurred speech, irritability or strange behaviour
- Heat stroke: fits, collapse or unconsciousness

## First aid steps
1. Move the person to a cool, shaded place immediately and loosen or remove extra clothing.
2. Cool them as fast as possible by pouring or sponging cool water over the body and fanning vigorously.
3. Place wet cloths or ice packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
4. If they are fully conscious and can swallow, give small sips of cool water, ORS, buttermilk, lassi or lemon water with salt.
5. Call 108 for anyone who is confused, collapses or does not improve within 30 minutes, and keep cooling them until help arrives.

## Do not
- Do not give fluids to a person who is unconscious, confused or having fits.
- Do not give alcohol, tea, coffee or very sugary drinks.
- Do not leave the person alone or send them back to work the same day.

## Prevention
- Do heavy field work in the early morning and late afternoon, and avoid working in direct sun between 12 noon and 3 pm on hot days.
- Drink water every 15 to 20 minutes while working, even when not thirsty.
- Wear loose, light-coloured cotton clothing and cover the head with a cloth, turban or hat.
- Take regular rest breaks in the shade, and watch co-workers, older people and children for early signs.
