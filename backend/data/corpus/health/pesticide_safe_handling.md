# Safe Handling and Spraying of Pesticides

Most pesticide poisoning among farmers is preventable. Exposure during mixing
and spraying, through the skin and by breathing spray mist, causes far more
cases than most people expect.

## Before spraying
- Read the label and leaflet, and use only the dose and crop listed on it.
- Check the coloured triangle on the label: red marks extremely toxic products, then yellow, blue and green in decreasing order of toxicity.
- Check the sprayer for leaks, especially at the tank lid, hose and nozzle.
- Wear protective gear: gloves, a mask, goggles, a full-sleeve shirt, long trousers and boots.

## While spraying
- Mix pesticide outdoors using a stick, never by hand.
- Spray in the cool hours of the morning or evening, walking so that the wind carries spray away from you.
- Do not spray in strong wind, in the heat of the day, or when rain is expected.
- Never eat, drink, smoke or chew tobacco while handling pesticide.
- Never clear a blocked nozzle by blowing into it. Use a soft brush or thin wire.

## After spraying
- Bathe with soap and change clothes immediately, and wash spray clothes separately from family laundry.
- Keep people and animals out of the sprayed field for the re-entry period given on the label.
- Observe the waiting period on the label before harvesting or letting animals graze.
- Store pesticides in their original containers, locked away from children, food and animal feed.
- Never reuse empty pesticide containers for water, food or grain. Puncture them and dispose of them as advised by the agriculture department.
