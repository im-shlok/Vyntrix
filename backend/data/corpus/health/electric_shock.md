# Electric Shock First Aid

Electric shock is a common cause of farm deaths in India, from faulty pump
sets and motor starters, bare or illegally hooked wires, wet switchboards, and
overhead lines that fall into fields during storms. The first rule is to
protect yourself: a rescuer who touches a person still in contact with live
current can be electrocuted too.

## First aid steps
1. Do not touch the person until the power is off. Switch off the main switch or motor starter, or remove the fuse or plug.
2. If you cannot switch it off, stand on something dry and push the person or wire away with a dry wooden stick or plank. Never use anything wet or metal.
3. For a fallen overhead or high-voltage line, stay far away, keep others away and call the electricity department. These lines cannot be moved safely with a stick.
4. Once the person is free, check whether they are breathing. If not, call 108 and start CPR if you are trained.
5. If they are breathing, lay them on their side, keep them warm, and cool any burns under clean running water for 20 minutes.
6. Take every electric shock victim to hospital, even if they seem fine, because current can disturb the heart rhythm hours later.

## Prevention
- Get pump-set wiring, starters and earthing checked by a licensed electrician, and fit an earth leakage circuit breaker (ELCB or RCCB) where possible.
- Keep switchboards dry and covered, and never operate them with wet hands or bare feet.
- Never hook wires directly onto overhead lines, and do not leave bare joints on cables running through wet fields.
- After storms, look for fallen or sagging lines before entering fields, and report them to the electricity department.
- Never connect a crop-protection fence directly to mains power. It is illegal and a frequent cause of human and animal deaths.
