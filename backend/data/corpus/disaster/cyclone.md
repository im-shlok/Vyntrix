# Cyclone Preparedness for Farmers

Cyclones strike India's east coast most often, especially Odisha, Andhra
Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal, and the Gujarat coast in the west, mainly
from April to June and from October to December. The India Meteorological
Department issues a pre-cyclone watch, followed by cyclone alerts and warnings,
usually several days before landfall. That lead time is enough to protect
lives and much of the produce.

## Steps
1. Follow IMD bulletins and district administration announcements, and move to a cyclone shelter as soon as you are told to evacuate.
2. Harvest mature crops early wherever possible, and move harvested produce, seed and fertiliser into raised, waterproof storage.
3. Move livestock to safe sheds on higher ground, or untie them so they can escape, rather than leaving them tied in the open.
4. Prop or tie banana plants, young orchard trees and tall crops, and secure loose tin sheets, polyhouse covers and equipment.
5. Stay indoors during the storm. If the wind suddenly drops, it may be the calm eye of the cyclone, and strong winds will return from the opposite direction.
6. After the cyclone, avoid fallen electric lines and damaged buildings, drain waterlogged fields, and record and report crop losses within 72 hours.

## Post-harvest losses
PM Fasal Bima Yojana covers losses from cyclone and cyclonic rain for crops
left to dry in the field in cut-and-spread condition, for up to 14 days after
harvest. Report such losses to the insurer within 72 hours.

## Sea water in fields
Storm surge can push sea water into coastal fields. Flush the salts out with
fresh water where drainage allows, get the soil tested before the next season,
apply amendments such as gypsum only if the laboratory recommends them, and
choose salt-tolerant varieties.
