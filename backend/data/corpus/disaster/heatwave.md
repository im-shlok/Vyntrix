# Heatwave Protection for Crops and Livestock

Heatwaves have become more frequent across north, central and eastern India,
mainly from March to June. Extreme heat during flowering and grain filling
cuts yields sharply, dries out the soil, and stresses livestock and workers.

## Steps
1. Irrigate lightly and often, preferably in the evening or early morning, to keep the root zone moist through heatwave days.
2. Mulch with crop residue or straw to cut evaporation and keep the soil cooler.
3. Do not spray pesticides or fertilisers in the heat of the day; spray only in the cool morning or evening.
4. Give livestock shade, plenty of clean drinking water and green fodder, and let buffaloes wallow or splash them during the hottest hours.
5. Schedule field work for the early morning and late afternoon, and watch workers for signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke.
6. Delay transplanting vegetable seedlings until the heatwave passes, or protect them with shade nets.

## Livestock under heat stress
Animals under heat stress pant, drool and stop eating, and milk yield drops.
Keep sheds ventilated, offer water several times a day, feed concentrates in
the cooler hours, and never tie animals in direct sun. Poultry are especially
vulnerable; use sprinklers or wet gunny curtains in poultry sheds.
