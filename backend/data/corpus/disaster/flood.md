# Flood Preparedness and Crop Damage Recovery

Floods and waterlogging damage kharif crops across large parts of India every
monsoon, especially in Assam, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal and Odisha.
Preparing before the water rises protects people, animals and stored produce,
and quick action afterwards limits crop loss and secures insurance claims.

## Steps
1. Move livestock, fodder, seed, fertiliser and farm machinery to higher ground before the water rises.
2. Switch off electricity to pump sets, motors and farm buildings, and stay away from fallen lines.
3. Never walk or drive through flowing water. Even knee-deep moving water can sweep an adult off their feet.
4. Keep an emergency kit ready: drinking water, dry food, a torch, a charged phone, medicines and important documents in a waterproof bag.
5. After the water recedes, drain fields as quickly as possible by opening bunds and cutting drainage channels.
6. Photograph the damage and report crop loss from inundation to your insurer, bank or agriculture office within 72 hours to protect a PM Fasal Bima Yojana claim.

## Assessing crop damage
Most field crops tolerate only short submergence. Crops submerged for more than
48 hours suffer severe root damage, and yield loss rises the longer the water
stands. Rice is the most tolerant, and submergence-tolerant varieties such as
Swarna-Sub1 can survive about two weeks under water. Vegetables, pulses, maize
and cotton are highly sensitive and may need resowing.

## Recovery after flooding
- Flooding washes nutrients out of the soil; ask the Krishi Vigyan Kendra about a corrective fertiliser dose once the field drains.
- Watch surviving crops for root rots and fungal diseases, and ask about contingency crops if resowing is needed.
- Drink only boiled or treated water, and wear boots in flood water and mud to reduce the risk of leptospirosis.
- Watch for snakes displaced by flood water in houses, stores and haystacks.
