# Lightning Safety for Farmers

Lightning kills more people in India than any other natural hazard, and
farmers working in open fields account for a large share of the deaths,
especially during pre-monsoon and monsoon thunderstorms. No place outdoors is
safe during a thunderstorm. The only real protection is to reach safe shelter
before the storm arrives.

## Warning signs
- Darkening storm clouds, a sudden strong wind and a drop in temperature
- Thunder of any kind: if you can hear it, you are close enough to be struck
- Lightning alerts on the Damini mobile app and IMD district nowcast messages

## Steps
1. Stop field work as soon as thunder is heard or a storm alert arrives, and go into a solid building or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows shut.
2. Stay away from isolated tall trees, electric poles, towers and wire fences.
3. Get off tractors and keep away from metal farm equipment, irrigation pipes and tube wells.
4. Leave ponds, canals and flooded paddy fields immediately and keep away from water.
5. If caught in the open with no shelter, squat low on the balls of your feet with feet together, head tucked and hands on knees. Do not lie flat on the ground. This is a last resort and does not make you safe.
6. Stay sheltered until 30 minutes after the last thunder.

## If someone is struck
A person struck by lightning carries no electric charge and is safe to touch.
Call 108 at once. If they are not breathing, start CPR if you are trained;
many lightning victims survive with prompt resuscitation. Check for burns and
other injuries, and take them to hospital.

## Protecting livestock
Move animals into a shed before a storm instead of tying them under trees or
near wire fences. Animals sheltering together under one tree are often killed
together by a single strike.
