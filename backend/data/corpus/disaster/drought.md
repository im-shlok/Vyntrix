# Drought and Dry Spell Management

A delayed monsoon, long dry spells within the season and early withdrawal of
rain all cause drought stress across rainfed India. Most rainfed districts have
an agriculture contingency plan, prepared by ICAR-CRIDA with the state
agricultural universities, that lists alternative crops and varieties for each
length of delay in rainfall.

## Steps
1. If the monsoon is delayed, switch to short-duration and drought-tolerant crops and varieties, such as millets, pulses and oilseeds, as listed in the district contingency plan.
2. Conserve soil moisture with mulching, inter-cultivation to break the surface crust, and compartmental bunding or ridge-and-furrow sowing.
3. Save the water you have for protective irrigation at the most sensitive stages, flowering and grain filling.
4. Use drip or sprinkler irrigation where possible, or irrigate alternate furrows to stretch scarce water.
5. Under severe stress, thin the crop to reduce plant population, and postpone top dressing of fertiliser until the soil has moisture.
6. Keep crop insurance and sowing records ready, and check for state drought relief announcements.

## Crop insurance in drought
Under PM Fasal Bima Yojana, drought and dry-spell losses are assessed for the
whole insurance unit through crop-cutting experiments rather than farm by farm.
When a severe mid-season adversity is declared, insured farmers can receive an
advance payment of up to 25% of the likely claim. Ask your bank or agriculture
office about notifications for your area.

## Long-term drought proofing
Farm ponds, check dams and the recharge of open wells capture monsoon runoff
for dry spells. Support for farm ponds and micro-irrigation is available under
the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana and through MGNREGA works in many
states.
